XContest points explained

Why one 40 km flight scores 40 points and another more than 60 – the five flight types of XContest, how the distance is measured and what makes a triangle “closed”.

The five flight types

Two questions decide which triangle you flew: what shape it has (flat or FAI) and how close you came back to where the triangle started (open or closed). If it is no triangle at all, it is a free flight.

Three words you need

  • Turnpoints – three points of your track that XContest picks as the corners of the triangle (TP1, TP2, TP3).
  • Perimeter – the three sides of the triangle added together: TP1 → TP2 → TP3 → back to TP1. It is the length of the red triangle in the drawings below, not a circle.
  • Gap – the shortest straight line between a point of your track before TP1 and a point after TP3. If you land near your launch, it is roughly the distance between the two. It is counted as a share of the perimeter.

Flat or FAI: the shape

Take the shortest side and divide it by the perimeter. At least 28 %: the triangle is an FAI triangle – fairly even, no side much shorter than the others. Below 28 %: it is a flat triangle – long and thin.

Open or closed: the gap

Divide the gap by the perimeter. At most 5 %: the triangle is closed. More than 5 % but at most 20 %: still a triangle, but open. More than 20 %: no triangle – the flight is scored as a free flight.

Shape and gap do not depend on each other: an FAI triangle can be open or closed, and so can a flat one.

How the points are calculated

Free flight: the points are the length of the line from start over up to three turnpoints to the end, in kilometres.

Triangle: scoring distance = perimeter − gap. Points = scoring distance in km × the number from the table.

XContest calculates every type for your flight and keeps the one with the most points.

What this means in the air

  • Closing pays: near the end of a triangle, the way back to the start can be worth more than kilometres anywhere else.
  • The 28 % rule shapes the route: a long, thin triangle is only flat (×1.2); spreading the turnpoints evenly makes it FAI (×1.4).
  • An out-and-return is a very flat triangle: if you land close enough to where you started – gap at most 20 % of the perimeter – it counts as a flat triangle. Land too far away and it is a free flight.

Questions

What is the difference between a flat and an FAI triangle?

The shape. In an FAI triangle every side is at least 28 % of the perimeter; otherwise it is a flat triangle. Open, an FAI triangle scores 1.4 points per km and a flat one 1.2.

When is a triangle closed?

When the gap – the shortest distance between your track before the first and after the third turnpoint – is at most 5 % of the perimeter. Closed triangles score more: 1.4 (flat) and 1.6 (FAI) points per km.

What is the perimeter of a triangle?

The three sides between the turnpoints added together. XContest subtracts the gap from it to get the scoring distance.

How many points per kilometre does XContest give?

Free flight 1.0, flat triangle 1.2, FAI triangle 1.4, closed flat triangle 1.4, closed FAI triangle 1.6 (XContest World rules).

Why is my out-and-return scored as a triangle?

An out-and-return is a very flat triangle. If you land close enough to where you started – gap at most 20 % of the perimeter – it counts as a flat triangle, which usually scores more than the free flight.

Good to know

These are the XContest World rules. National cups and leagues can use other coefficients or rules. The official score is the one XContest shows after your upload. Tracktale computes the same flight types to show the route and the points on your cards; it is an approximation and can differ slightly from XContest.
